While self-reviewing #2375 (the mypy `--` separator fix), one of the two spawned reviewers (Explore) returned a final message that fully stated its one finding (a sibling bug in `validators/pyright/pyright.py` -- the same missing-`--` shape, though pyright's CLI does not honor `--` at all, confirmed empirically). The reviewer's message opened with `FINDINGS: 1` and enumerated the finding in full, plus a separate "Clean areas checked" bullet list below it.

Running this through this plugin's own `scripts/review_return.py --framed` classifier (from a checkout of Digital-Process-Tools/claude-oss, path `/Users/floriandavid/Documents/claude-oss/scripts/review_return.py`) returned `VERDICT: referred-not-stated`, exit 3 -- meaning "the message points at material it does not carry."

Root-caused by reading the classifier's own regex source. The `_BACKREF` pattern includes an alternative matching one of a closed list of verbs (reported, report, found, listed, list, described, detailed, noted, note, mentioned, stated, outlined, flagged, identified, documented, given, shown), followed by up to three words, followed by one of (above, earlier, previously, already).

It matched the ordinary English phrase "found already installed" inside one of the reviewer's "Clean areas checked" bullets ("Verified against the real `mypy` 2.3.1 binary (found already installed in a scratch venv on this machine)...") -- `found` ... `already`, treated by the regex as a self-referential gesture like "found above" or "reported already", even though the sentence has nothing to do with referring back to unstated findings. The finding itself was completely and correctly stated in the message; only the mechanical classification was wrong.

Verified directly by re.search-ing the exact match span in the pasted message text: `span=(2977, 2990), match='found already'` -- confirming this specific substring, not the finding block itself, drove the `referred-not-stated` classification.

Cost: one full re-spawn of the Explore reviewer (per `agents/developer/review-return.md`'s "one permitted re-spawn" rule) to get a second, differently-worded message that avoided the trigger words, purely to satisfy the mechanical classifier -- the underlying review was never actually lost.

Not deciding here whether this belongs in a jit-context rule, whether the fix is a narrower regex (e.g. requiring the referenced noun to be "findings"/"analysis"/"review"/"above" text rather than any verb+"already" pairing), or whether it is a below-bar edge case not worth hardening. Logging for the curator and, separately, reporting upstream to the oss plugin's own tracker as a plugin-tooling defect per the "declared dependency" clause in agents/developer.md.
