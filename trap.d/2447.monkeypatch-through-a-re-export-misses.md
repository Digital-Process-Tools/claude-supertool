# A monkeypatch through a re-export reaches a name the function never reads

Observed while moving `presets/git/_git_common.py`'s git invocation layer to
`presets/_git_run.py` (#2447) and leaving `from _git_run import ...` behind as a
re-export.

`tests/test_git_common_lock_wait_2034.py` loads `_git_common.py` and does
`monkeypatch.setattr(mod, "_lock_fd_holder", ...)` before calling
`mod._diagnose_lock(...)`. Both names are on `_git_common` after the move, so the
patch appears to work and the call appears to be the right one. It is not:
`_diagnose_lock`'s body resolves `_lock_fd_holder` in `_git_run`'s globals, which
the patch never touched. The stub was ignored and the real `lsof` scan ran.

What made it nearly invisible: of the three assertions, two kept passing.

    monkeypatch.setattr(mod, "_lock_fd_holder", lambda path, **k: True)
    assert "live" in mod._diagnose_lock(str(lock))     # passed, WRONGLY

The real scan returned False, so the verdict was `stale` -- and the stale
verdict's own prose reads "how a live write gets corrupted instead of a stale one
cleared". The word `live` is in it. An unanchored substring assertion passed on
the opposite verdict.

Only the third state failed, which is how it was found at all:

    AssertionError: assert 'cannot tell' in 'lock-diagnosis: stale -- ...'

Cost: one confused re-read of a test that passed on master and failed on the
branch, before noticing the two assertions above it had gone vacuous rather than
the one below being broken.

Fixed by pointing `PRESET` at `presets/_git_run.py` (patch the module the
function actually reads, not the one that re-exports it) and by anchoring all
three assertions on `lock-diagnosis: <verdict>` rather than the bare word.

Two lessons and I am not sure which is the rule:

1. A re-export is a second binding. Patch where the body resolves the name.
2. An `in` assertion against prose that discusses its own sibling states is a
   false pass waiting to happen. Anchor on the field, not the word.

Probably both, and (2) is the one that generalises past this file.
