- **`radar` gained a `gl-issue` tier — a board scoped to one GitLab issue, not a population** ([#898](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/898)). Every existing tier answers "how is my whole population" (`gl-mrs`: every open MR; `gh-prs`: every open PR); nothing answered "how is issue #12657" — its own related MRs, its label changes, its reopens. `{"radar_tiers": {"gl-issue": {}}}` plus `radar:gl-issue:12657` reports exactly that: the issue's state and labels, every one of its still-`opened` related MRs with a `gitlab-mr` watcher healed onto it, and — from the second run on, once there is a previous snapshot to diff against — a reopen, a label change, or a related MR that arrived or departed, each counted against `healthy` for the same reason a departed MR counts against `gl-mrs`'s own health (`quiet_when_healthy` must not be able to silence the one thing this tier exists to surface). No feed poller and no filter vocabulary: the population is one live query scoped to the issue's own id, so there is nothing to discover between runs that a fresh query would not already show. Deliberately does not attempt the issue's fuller vision — a new human-event source, per-tier/per-source policy markdown, or a per-category history ledger — all named as follow-up rather than built here, since the policy-and-registry mechanism the issue's own comments describe is meant to be shared with the separate `dashboard` op (#953) rather than decided twice.
