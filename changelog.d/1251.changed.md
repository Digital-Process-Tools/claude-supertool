- **A changelog fragment must now name its own issue, and the release check refuses one that does not** ([#1251](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1251)). `changelog.d/<issue>.<section>.md` holds the issue number in exactly one structural place — the filename — and assembly writes the *body* into `CHANGELOG.md` and deletes the file. So the number survived a release only when the author happened to type it into the prose, which made findability a property of author habit rather than of the release process.

  Measured on the fragment bodies as they stood at each release commit: **8 of the 20 entries in v0.32.0** and **6 of the 28 in v0.33.0** named every issue but their own, several citing three or four neighbours. Only two of the twenty had a `test_the_change_is_findable` to say so, and the two that did are why the defect was found at all — the other six would have shipped in silence. One of the eight, #1197, was findable only because a *different* fragment in the same release happened to mention it.

  `collect()` gains a `self_reference_finding` stage and the `changelog-fragment` validator mirrors it, so `paste` and `edit` refuse the fragment at write time and `--check` refuses it in the PR. `(#1251)` anywhere in the body satisfies it, as does a link whose URL ends in the number; the `- **Bold summary** ([#1251](link)).` opening `changelog.d/README.md` has always prescribed does both. A bare `1251` does not, deliberately: v0.32.0's #1130 was findable only because it cited `tests/test_preset_git_splitlines_register_1130.py`, which no reader would aim at and no author could be told to produce.

  The alternative was to have the assembler append the reference itself. It was refused on two grounds: it would rewrite an authored entry, and `changelog.d/README.md` promises that nothing is reformatted; and its unavoidable "is it already there?" test cannot distinguish a self-citation from the #1197 coincidence.

  The stage sits ahead of the body scan because it needs no parser — a definite refusal must not be lost behind the `CannotValidate` raised when `markdown-it-py` is absent — but it does not stop the scan, so a fragment that is both malformed and silent about its issue gets both findings in one run.
