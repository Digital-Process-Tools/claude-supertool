- A post-edit lint that blew its wall-clock budget no longer reddens the suite as though the product had failed ([#1360](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1360)). `test_xml_lint` broke `pytest (windows-latest, 3.10)` on PR #1355 with one failure in 9711: the product declined correctly — `POST-EDIT LINT TIMED OUT — xmllint (30s)`, naming the knob — and the test converted that decline into a red leg. That is this repo's own defect class relocated into the thing meant to detect it. [#553](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/553) is the same failure at a 5s budget, so raising the number a third time buys an interval of quiet against an unbounded tail, not a fix.
- Three states, not two: `tests/_lint_budget.py` sits in front of a verdict assertion and returns when the checker reached a verdict, **skips** carrying `lint-budget(#1360)` when it timed out, and **fails** otherwise. `conftest` prints the count, its denominator and its population every run including at zero, so `N skipped` on a Windows leg resolves to `N did not reach a lint verdict here` rather than reading as coverage.
- The skip is deliberately narrow, because the alternative trades the loud bug for the quiet one. A `POST-EDIT LINT DECLINED — could not start the checker (FileNotFoundError)` is [#997](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/997)'s Windows-only spawn failure and stays red; so does a *timeout* naming a budget that is not the one `SUPERTOOL_LINT_TIMEOUT` resolves to, which is the knob not reaching the subprocess — a product bug wearing the flake's clothes, told apart without guessing because the receipt states the budget it used.
- Applied to the class, not the instance: seven sites across `.php`, `.xml` and `.py` assert a verdict from a subprocess-backed checker, and `py_compile` spawns a fresh interpreter — the more expensive of the two. `tests/test_lint_budget_gating_1360.py` derives that population from the AST rather than listing it, asserts every member is gated, and asserts the population has not collapsed to nothing, which is how a hand-listed register passes vacuously ([#1232](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1232), [#1274](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1274)). "Gated" means a real **call** to the gate **above** the first verdict assertion — #1232's `_called_before` lesson, and both halves are load-bearing: a gate written below the assertion it guards is #1360 verbatim, since the assertion runs first.
- The gate reads `_supertool._LINT_TIMEOUT_PREFIX`, now named in the product and used by the branch that emits it, instead of matching a copied message. A predicate that sniffs prose to tell an environment limit from a finding is one reword away from swallowing the finding.
- `pytest_terminal_summary` no longer returns early when one gating helper is absent from the tree, which would have printed the symlink verdict and silently omitted the lint one — the same absence, one layer up.
