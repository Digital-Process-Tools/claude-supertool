- **`git-push` no longer launches its recovery on an allowance nothing completes in, then reports the result as a timeout** ([#1649](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1649)). Since [#1615](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1615) the recovery fetch, rebase and re-push draw from the push deadline instead of spending 240s beside it — correct, and it left the decline firing at exactly zero. A push that spent 295s of its 300s budget and was *then* rejected non-fast-forward launched the fetch on **5s**, timed out, and produced `fetch TIMED OUT (5s)`: true about the fetch, and misleading about the cause, since nothing was ever going to finish. The caller then raises a number that never cut.

  **The floor is 30s, and it is derived rather than picked.** It is what this op already gives `git ls-remote` — one network round-trip to the same remote, ref advertisement and nothing else — and a recovery fetch is that round-trip plus negotiation plus a packfile, so it cannot be worth less. Measured against github.com from a fast link on 2026-08-14: an already-current `git fetch origin master` took 1.77–2.02s, and one that actually transferred (`--depth 200` onto a `--depth 1` clone) 4.33s. 5s sits inside the noise of the good case.

  **Declining, not clamping.** Below the floor the step is not attempted and the receipt says how many seconds were left, what the minimum is, and that a retry gets a fresh budget — the third state this file argues for everywhere else (`docs/validators.md`, "Declining instead of guessing"). "The budget was spent" and "5s of 300 were left" are now two different sentences, because they send the caller to two different numbers.

  Reported as reasoned from the code rather than observed — the reproduction needs a diverged remote and a slow push. The arm is reachable: it is pinned by a test that stubs the remaining budget and drives a real non-fast-forward through a bare-remote sandbox.
