- **`read` announced the byte cap had cut a window short when it had returned every byte, contradicting the byte-exact remedy `grep` prints to send callers there** ([#1616](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1616)). `grep` names `read:PATH:N-N` as the way back to a line it truncated ([#1489](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1489)). The read delivered all 25,006 bytes and then said `stopping at line 2: cut short by the 20000-byte cap and the limit was reached coincide here — which one ended the window cannot be told apart`, with a footer offering `truncated at 20000 bytes ... (2 more lines)` for lines nobody had asked for. The cap is tested *after* a whole line is emitted, so it never truncates a line — it drops the ones that would have followed, and when the loop stopped at the window's own end there were none to drop.

  **Reaching the cap and being cut by it are two facts now.** Only a read that stopped short of its own limit and of EOF is reported as cut; a window that ended anyway says so, and the cap is disclosed as a fact instead — `the 20000-byte cap was reached on that line and dropped nothing; it stops whole lines and never truncates one, so these bytes are complete`. Silence would have been the other half of the same defect: the output *is* at the cap, and a wider window will lose lines. A cap that genuinely did drop lines still says `cut short`, pinned by its own test.

  **The `grep=` branch made the same misattribution one render over**, and is fixed with it. A filtered read whose scan stopped at the cap correctly says the remaining lines were never searched — but when the LIMIT ended the scan on a line that happened to carry the total over the cap, the unsearched lines are the limit's and the note credited them to the cap, telling the caller to widen the wrong bound. Found reviewing this change, not by the issue.
