- **A rolled-back write through a symlink no longer deletes the symlink and keeps the bytes** ([#1136](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1136)). `_atomic_write` follows a symlink to its target on purpose, so `paste:link.py` writes `target.py`. The rollback sampled `os.path.isfile(path)` and called `os.unlink(path)` on the *unresolved* path: for a link whose target did not exist yet, `isfile` followed the link, found nothing, and the rollback read that as "this call created link.py". It then removed a symlink the call never created, left the `target.py` it really had written, and printed `nothing changed on disk` over both.

  **Identity is now decided on the path the writer lands on**, sampled once before the op and reused by every rollback arm, through one `_write_target` helper that `_atomic_write` itself calls — two hand-written copies of `realpath if islink` is how the two sides came apart, and it is also what makes the platform differences in `os.path.islink` harmless. The three states are unchanged: a target that existed is still restored rather than unlinked. The retraction line names the target and says the link is intact, because `link.py removed` was accurate only while the wrong object was being deleted.
