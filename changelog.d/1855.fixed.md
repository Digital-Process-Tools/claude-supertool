- **`edit`'s refusal printed `nearest match at lines 114-125 (100%)` directly under `old string not found`** ([#1855](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1855)). Two sentences a caller cannot both believe. The issue reasoned that some normalisation was making the strings equal; measured, nothing was. `SequenceMatcher.ratio()` returns `0.9980013324450366` for a twelve-line block differing by three trailing spaces, and `f"{0.998:.0%}"` is `100%` — the metric was honest and the **render** rounded a near miss up into a claim of identity. So it is a receipt defect, and the fix is one message rather than the matcher.

  A near miss now reports `>99%`, and the receipt names **what kind** of difference it is — `differs only in whitespace` or `differs in 1 of 12 lines` — because the caller's question is never "how close" but "what do I look for". The reported cost was one extra read call spent finding a one-line difference the scan had already measured and thrown away.

  **The genuine 100% is kept and explained rather than capped.** `splitlines()` drops line endings and the hint strips blank lines off both ends of the anchor, so an anchor can be equal line-for-line to a window the byte comparison rejected. Capping that would be a lie in the other direction, so it reads `identical line-for-line, so the difference is outside the compared text: line endings, or blank lines at the ends of `old``. Leaving it bare would have left the filed contradiction alive in the one case where the number really is 100.

  The comparison itself is untouched, asserted by a control: an anchor that is in the file still matches, writes, and produces no hint at all.
