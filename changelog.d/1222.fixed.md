- **`unknown operation` listed 44 builtins, none of which was the answer, and withheld the 50 loaded ops that held it** ([#1222](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1222)). `supertool 'worktrees'` printed the builtin roster and then told the reader to make a second call to see the preset ops — one of which was `git-worktrees`. The name was already in the process; only the printing was missing. The refusal now carries a `Did you mean:` line above the roster, produced by three rules and nothing else: a documented synonym, a candidate that is exactly `<prefix>-<typed>` (`worktrees` → `git-worktrees`), and one edit — insert, delete, substitute, or an adjacent swap, so `raed` finds `read` — over names of four characters or more. Under four characters a single edit is noise (`lsx` is one from `ls`) and nothing is offered, because a wrong name costs the reader a round-trip that silence would not have. Candidates are the ops loaded in that process, capped at three; a shipped preset this cwd does not enable is named only when nothing loaded matches, and is labelled `not loaded here`. The roster is never replaced by the suggestion — the guess can still be wrong and the list cannot.
- **A wrong-case op name was answered with its neighbour** ([#1222](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1222), found reviewing the first commit). `Read:x` produced `Did you mean: head` — lowercased, `Read` is distance 0 from `read`, which the "a name is not a suggestion for itself" guard discarded, leaving `head` at distance 1 as the top candidate. An exact case-insensitive match is now the first rule and returns alone, because once the name is known a runner-up is noise. `blame` also survived in `tests/test_parallel.py` and in a `test_parallel_safe_writes_1244.py` docstring that named it as an example of an op absent from `_OP_SAFETY_BUILTIN` — it was absent because it had stopped being an op, which is the one thing that check cannot see.
