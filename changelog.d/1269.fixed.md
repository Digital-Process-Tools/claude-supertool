- **Four ops accepted the `force` bypass and did not say so in the registry** ([#1269](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1269)). `bluesky_like`, `bluesky_follow`, `devto_publish` and `hashnode_publish` all read a `force` token out of their argument, and none of them named it in the `syntax` string that `supertool 'ops'` prints. `force` is the bypass for the publish confirmation gate and for the fail-closed pre-flight duplicate checks ([#562](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/562), [#601](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/601)) — the refusal tells the caller to use it, and the registry did not tell them it exists. Three of the four declared it in their own module docstring and all four in the prose of `docs/presets/*.md`; the registry was the one surface that omitted it, and it is the one an agent reads.

  A new guard sweeps every op in every manifest, decides by AST whether the script *evaluates* the string `force` (a docstring or an error message mentioning `|force` does not count, or the sweep would be about prose), and requires the `syntax` string to name it as a whole word — `git-push:force-with-lease` is a different token. **10 ops read it; 4 did not declare it.** An op whose `cmd` cannot be resolved to a script raises rather than dropping out of the sweep.

  The guard is deliberately one token wide, and says why in its own docstring. The other two routes #1269 proposed were measured against this registry first: deriving tokens from the parse yields 199 candidates of which 4 are real, 2% precision; round-tripping every token the `syntax` names finds 0 drifts across 34 ops and 56 tokens, and executing that check for real would mean invoking the confirmation-bypass token on the write ops it most matters for.
