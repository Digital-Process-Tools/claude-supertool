- A capped comment list in `gh-issue` and `gh-pr` keeps the first three comments and the last seven, instead of the ten most recent ([#738](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/738)). The budget is unchanged at ten, so no caller pays more context for it.

- The two sides of #738 are right about different comments — the head carries the objection that opened the thread, the tail carries the resolution that closed it — so a cap that takes one end guarantees that on every long thread one of the two load-bearing regions is gone, and the reader cannot tell which. A thread whose opening never mattered rendered identically to one whose opening was the whole point.

- The measurement that would have settled which end is not available, and that is the argument rather than a gap in it: measured across the whole tracker on 2026-08-11 the busiest thread in this repository has six comments and the cap has never fired here, so there is no corpus in which to count where the load-bearing comment sits. With no evidence for either end, the design that does not require choosing one is the answer. It is also not a new convention — `gl-mr` has kept a head and a recency tail with an inline gap marker since it was written.

- The disclosure moved with the cut. The header reads `10 of 25 shown, 15 hidden from the middle` rather than `15 earlier truncated`, which was true of a tail-only cap and would be false of this one; and the seam itself now carries `...[15 comments hidden here — use :full to fetch all]`, because a header alone leaves the reader unable to see that two adjacent comments are not consecutive.
