- **`_repo_target`'s marker tuples are documented as narrower than the call sites, because they are** ([#1807](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1807)). `_ABSENT_CWD` and `_ABSENT_TARGET` claimed to hold *the same phrases the twelve classifying call sites already match on*. They hold strict refinements of those phrases: `no git remotes` and `git remotes found` for the call sites' bare `git remotes`, `could not determine base repository` for their `could not determine`, and no counterpart at all for their broadest marker, `not found`.

  The narrowing is deliberate, and the comment now says why rather than claiming the two lists are one. A call site is an ordered if-chain over a single lookup: its `repo` arm is tried before `notfound`, `auth` and `ratelimit`, and only after the caller has turned a missing binary and a timeout into their own messages. `classify_detail` is a standalone two-way classifier with no ordering and nothing filtered out ahead of it, so the same words carry a different risk — widening it to the call sites' vocabulary would classify `gh not found - install from ...` as *absent*, which is [#1789](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1789) reintroduced in the module written to fix it.

  The cost of staying narrow is stated too: a future `gh` rewording that satisfies only a broad marker lands on `unknown` here and `absent` at a call site. That is a real divergence, in the direction that asserts nothing false about the reader's machine.

  `tests/test_repo_target_marker_vocabulary_1807.py` derives both vocabularies from the tree with an AST register rather than retyping them, and fails if the tuples grow a spelling no call site matches, if the strict pairs stop being strict, or if the comment goes back to claiming the lists are the same. No behaviour changed.
