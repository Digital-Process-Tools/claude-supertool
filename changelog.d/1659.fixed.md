- **`git-push`'s `:budget=SECONDS` is now checked against the op timeout your project set, not only against the module constant** ([#1659](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1659)). #1631 made `ops.git-push.timeout` a value a repository chooses and taught the config path to validate `ops.git-push.budget` against the merged entry; the flag path was left checking `_PUSH_TIMEOUT_MAX = 1800` alone. A project writing `{"ops": {"git-push": {"timeout": 60}}}` could still ask for `:budget=1700` and be accepted — 28x its own op cap — and supertool then killed the child at 60s, which is exactly the outcome the flag's own refusal text cited: a bare `FAIL (timeout)` for a push that may have landed ([#399](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/399)), and on the recovery path the loss of the receipt that would have said the worktree is paused mid-rebase ([#1615](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1615)).

  **Both ceilings apply, and the smaller one binds.** They are not two spellings of one rule, which is why neither is now dead: `ops.git-push.timeout` is where supertool kills the process, and 1800 is the longest this op will make a caller wait — a repository raising its own timeout has said something about the first and nothing about the second. The rule lives in one function, `_budget_ceiling_refusal`, used by the flag path and the config path alike; two copies of it is what produced the asymmetry.

  **Two false sentences went with it.** The flag's refusal said the ceiling "has to stay strictly under `ops.git-push.timeout` **in presets/git.json**" — a claim about a value #1631 had just made a repository's to override, sending whoever read it to edit the shipped preset instead of their own config. And the `v0.42.0` entry for #1631 said the config path was "matching what `_parse_budget` already does for the flag". It was not, at the time it was written; it is now. That entry stands as the record of what v0.42.0 shipped rather than being edited after the fact.
