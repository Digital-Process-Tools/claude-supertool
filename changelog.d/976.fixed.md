- **The refname AST guard (`tests/test_forged_branch_line_965.py`) now catches 12 of the 14 non-trivial print shapes named in #976's own audit, not 4** ([#976](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/976)). Its marker check used to clear an entire expression when a call to `flat()`/`fence()`/etc. appeared ANYWHERE in it, so `flat(t) + d.get('headRefName')` inside one f-string laundered the unflattened access sitting right beside the marked one (`H_marker_in_same_expr`, #976's own "the dangerous one"); the check is now scoped to a marker call's own arguments (`_iter_unmarked`). The scanner also now scans a whole `print()`/`sys.stdout.write()` argument for a direct refname read regardless of shape -- concatenation, `%`-formatting, `.format()`, a second `print()` argument -- rather than only the inside of an f-string, and tracks a direct `.get()`/subscript read through tuple-unpack and `AugAssign` the same way a plain assignment already was. Two of the fourteen remain a documented boundary rather than a silent drop: following a value across a call into a DIFFERENT function's own locals, and a dict key read out of a variable rather than typed as a literal -- both are the step from "which syntactic shapes carry a value read directly, in this same function" into a general taint tracker this scanner has never tried to be. A third widening -- chasing an already-tainted NAME through further computation, not just re-display -- was tried and reverted after it chained taint through an unrelated regex match's `.group()` result in `presets/gitlab/job.py` onto locals that never carry the raw refname text. `presets/git/worktrees.py::_pr_detail` is fixed directly at the source (one of the two live instances #976 named, chased down and confirmed): it built a `baseRefName` read into a printed board row through two hops of local variable flow the scanner deliberately does not chase. `presets/_untrusted.py`'s own coverage claim is rewritten to name the boundary rather than only describe the mechanism.
