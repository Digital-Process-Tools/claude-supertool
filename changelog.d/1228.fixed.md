- `git-commit` now commits **only the paths you name**, instead of the whole index ([#1228](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1228)). It ran `git add <paths>` and then a pathspec-less `git commit`, so anything another process had staged rode in: on 2026-08-09 a review agent's staged revert was swept into a commit that silently un-did 139 lines of a production fix, while the worktree still held the correct file and the tests still passed against the worktree. The pathless call (`git-commit:::MESSAGE`, no PATHS) is unchanged and still commits what you staged by hand — that is the spelling that means it. During a merge or cherry-pick the pathspec stands down, because git refuses a partial commit there outright.
- The receipt now names what was **staged and left out** of the commit, read from the index after the commit rather than from the working tree. The original incident printed `2 modified tracked file(s) were NOT included` naming the two files it had just committed — a contradiction the author had to `git diff` to resolve, and the only reason it was caught at all.
