- An adapter can now state how its `count` relates to its `errors`, and the core refuses to measure a payload that contradicts its own statement (#1728). `_validator_measured_count` is `max(count - absences, len(rows) - absences, 0)`, and the two terms serve two different adapters: the floor stops one whose `count` already excludes its `adapter` stall rows being subtracted from twice, and subtracting from `count` rather than recomputing from `errors` stops a capped list reading fifty findings as five. Neither survives a payload whose `count` is bounded by the same cap that bounds `errors` — `count=4` over four findings and a stall row on both sides of an edit that added a fifth finding measures 4 to 4, so `_validator_regressed` returns false and a `rollback_on_fail` validator does not revert over a genuinely new finding. Nothing ships that shape (`cargo-check` and `phpstan` were both checked and neither caps), and no arithmetic can close it, because a cap is invisible in a single payload unless the adapter says so: every candidate change trades this blind spot for the one `test_a_capped_error_list_is_not_read_as_a_smaller_count` already pins. So the fix is a contract. `count_basis` (`total` or `measured`) and `errors_truncated` are declared together or not at all; an adapter may pre-subtract its stall rows **or** cap its list and not both; `count` may never be truncated by the same cap as `errors`, nor fall below the rows printed under it. A payload breaking any of those is published as an `adapter`-coded fault naming the numbers — `NOT CHECKED`, kept out of the delta, never a rollback, still exiting non-zero — instead of a comparison nobody can read, and never as `skipped`, which would be quieter than the defect it reports. Declaring stays optional at runtime, since a repo's own validator named in `.supertool.json` is part of this population and a mandate would break every third-party adapter on upgrade; it is mandatory for a shipped adapter, held by a set of the 34 that do not yet declare which may only shrink. `cargo-check` (`total`) and `phpstan` (`measured`) declare, because they are the two the core cites as the divergent conventions and so the two the next adapter is copied from — an accident that holds is not a guarantee. Two limits are in the docs rather than implied: declaring changes no number for a conforming payload, so no shipped adapter's counts move, and an adapter that caps while declaring `measured` is indistinguishable from a well-formed `measured` payload within one payload, which is why this is a statement an author makes and not a property the core infers.
