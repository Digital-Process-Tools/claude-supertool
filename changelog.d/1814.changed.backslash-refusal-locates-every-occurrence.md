- **A doubled-backslash refusal now locates every occurrence, with a payload line, a column and a caret** ([#1814](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1814), [#1808](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1808), [#1819](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1819)). The gate was never the problem — every reported refusal was correct and at least two caught real payload bugs. The cost of *answering* one was: the retry on this route is the whole payload, and a refusal that named the first of three offences bought three full re-sends of a file the scanner had already parsed in full. Measured at four re-sends in one agent run, on payloads up to 14 KB. Within a field the scan walked every pair to produce its count and then discarded every position but the first; `search` became `finditer` and the positions are now reported. Each is rendered as `N/M at payload line L, column C` with an excerpt of the caller's own line **centred on the pair** rather than up to 48 characters of the head of whatever line it sat on — on the reported 9 KB payload with 93 pairs, the head of the line did not contain the offending bytes at all, because a shell `printf` format is frequently 200 characters in. The message also says the two fixes are **opposite** and that nothing in the tool can tell which was meant: *needs `literal_backslashes = true`* and *has doubled backslashes that wanted to be single* are decided per occurrence and used to read identically. Beyond four occurrences in a field, and beyond three fields, the remainder is **named by payload line, never merely counted**. Where the excerpt must be flattened (a `U+2028` in the value, #1583) the caret is declined with a reason rather than drawn on offsets the flattening has shifted — the line and column are measured on the raw payload and stay exact.
