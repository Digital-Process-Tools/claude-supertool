- **The AST census that enforces `use_utf8_stdout()` now also flags a `print()` argument it cannot judge, instead of silently treating it as clean** ([#2065](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/2065)). `tests/test_encoding_seam.py`'s `non_ascii_print_literals` matched non-ASCII string literals reaching `print`, and `_untrusted` covered interpolated values, but `presets/classify/check.py` printed `print(run(text))` -- computed, carrying a matched fence glyph at runtime, and caught by neither. The census reported `literals=[] pin_state=unpinned`, the same reading a genuinely ASCII-only file gets. A new `computed_print_sites()` detector closes the specific gap that crashed (a `print()` argument that is not a plain string literal); a new test asserts it, positive and negative fixtures included. Whether to widen the *hard-failing* enumerator (`test_every_preset_printing_a_non_ascii_literal_pins_its_stdout`) to also fail on every computed print was measured rather than assumed before deciding: 41 of 103 preset entry points -- about 4 in 10 -- have an unpinned `print()` call whose argument is not a literal. That is too large a number to triage as a rider on a coverage fix, and #2065 itself names the risk of doing it anyway (a guard that fires on 4 in 10 files trains people to add the pin without thinking). `computed_print_sites()` is exercised and its current count is pinned as a report (`test_the_glyph_scan_reports_what_it_cannot_verify`), so the gap stays visible and the pinning decision for those 41 entry points is left to a follow-up their own authors can weigh.
