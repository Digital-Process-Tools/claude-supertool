- **`git-push:budget=N` was spent twice on the rebase-recovery path, so above ~840s the ceiling that guarantees a verdict did not hold** ([#1615](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1615)). `_push_budget()` was handed to `git push` at both push sites — the initial attempt and the re-push after a clean rebase — with `_RECOVER_TIMEOUT` for the fetch and again for the rebase in between, and nothing accounting for the time already spent. Worst case `2N + 240` against `ops.git-push.timeout = 1920`, so any `N > 840` could reach supertool's outer cap; `:budget=1800` asked for 3840s. Past that cap the process is killed, on the recovery path, where `_report_recovery_timeout` is the only thing that would have told the caller their worktree is paused mid-rebase — the outcome `_parse_budget`'s own ceiling text says the ceiling exists to prevent ([#399](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/399)). The default (300, so 840 total) was safe; this arrived with the caller-supplied budget in [#1530](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1530).

  **`:budget=N` is now a deadline on this op's pushing, not a per-call timeout.** The clock opens at the first `git push` and the recovery fetch, the rebase and the re-push each take the smaller of their own budget and what is left of it. The single-push case is unchanged to the second — the preamble that picks a remote and the receipt that reads the result stay outside the clock, the receipt deliberately, because an expiring clock past the point of no return must never cost the caller the verdict ([#675](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/675)).

  **What it costs, since a deadline and a per-call timeout are different contracts and this is the trade:** a first push that spends most of `N` and is *then* rejected non-fast-forward now leaves little or nothing for the recovery. That recovery is **declined rather than run short** — a third state, `NOT PUSHED - BUDGET SPENT`, naming whether the rebase had already replayed the branch, because a `git push` launched on an expired clock is killed before it can verify anything and the verdict is the whole product. Previously it ran for another `N + 240` and could be killed with no receipt at all.

  **The receipt for a stall nobody can attribute now names no number at all.** `_report_recovery_timeout`'s third caller is a backstop wrapping the whole recovery in `except subprocess.TimeoutExpired`, for calls that do not report their own stage — it knows a call stalled, not which one. It used to default to `_RECOVER_TIMEOUT`, which was safe while that constant *was* the clock on every call it covers; it no longer is, so a number there would be a budget nothing was measured against. Three states for the receipt's own field, on the same terms as everything else here. Found reviewing this change, not by the issue.

  The timeout receipt reads the clock the push was actually launched with rather than the budget it was cut from, and says which is which when they differ — a receipt quoting a clock that did not cut sends the caller to raise a number they never reached, which is #1530's own defect one indirection further in. `_report_recovery_timeout` does the same for `_RECOVER_TIMEOUT`.
