- **The suite's symlink-capability line reported a floor as a total** ([#1274](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1274)). `conftest` printed `N symlink-dependent tests did NOT run on this platform`, counting only skips carrying the `symlink-capability(#1143)` token. Three of the five mechanisms that keep a symlink call site off a privilege-less runner produce no such skip, and the register counted 27 sites under them, not the 20 the issue estimated -- 12 hand-rolled probes and 8 unrelated platform markers, which skip without the token, plus 7 `except OSError` arms, which do not skip at all -- so `N` was a subset presented as a total. [#1232](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1232) had fixed the loud version of this, where the line read `0` while four tests were failing for exactly that reason; a plausible non-zero number is quieter and prompts nobody to check it. Twelve of those sites were removable and are removed: the three `watch` hostile-file suites each hand-rolled a `_can_symlink()` probe with their own `pytest.skip` wording, and now call the shared `require_symlink()`, so their skips are counted. The other two mechanisms are kept deliberately -- a `needs_nofollow` marker fires at collection on every Windows runner whether or not the privilege is present, and a symlink call inside an `except OSError` arm is not a skip at all -- so stamping the token onto them would assert a reason nothing measured. Instead the line now carries its own population: `N of M skipped tests did NOT run for this reason`, followed by which mechanisms are not in `N` and where the full list lives. Observed under a forced-absent privilege in a child pytest; `windows-latest` has the privilege today, so the privilege-less runner remains unobserved from here.
