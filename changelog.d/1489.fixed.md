- **`edit`'s nearest-match hint scored the first non-blank line of the anchor and nothing else, so a multi-line anchor was pointed at whichever copy of its boilerplate came first.** The run that filed [#1489](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1489) was sent to a line ~800 away from the correct one, at an identical 68%, twice: `SequenceMatcher.ratio()` of a `def` line is a fact about that `def` line, and every copy of it in the file scores the same. Raising the floor was the wrong repair — the number is not too low, it is measuring something else — so the metric moved instead. A multi-line anchor is now located by a sliding multiset of whitespace-normalised lines over every window of the anchor's height (O(file)), then char-scored on the top-ranked windows only, and the receipt names the window: `nearest match at lines 804-806 (91%): read:app.py:804-806`.

- **The hint has a third state.** Where two or more positions score within 2 points of the leader, `cannot suggest a nearest match: N places score the same (91%) — lines 1, 44` replaces the coin flip, because a confidently wrong line number costs more than no line number: the caller reads the wrong 30 lines and re-anchors against them (#1489).

- **The same scan could not be finished on a minified file, and said nothing about it.** `_EDIT_DIAG_MAX_LINES` bounds the diagnostic by line COUNT — the one dimension a minified bundle is small in — while `SequenceMatcher.ratio()` is O(len(a) × len(b)). Measured on 60 lines of 40 KB with a 39 KB anchor: 220s for a single comparison and over 30 minutes for the file, on a courtesy that runs on a failure path. Each comparison is now clipped to 1000 characters, the total is capped in cells, and a percentage computed over a clipped line says `scored on the first 1000 characters` rather than presenting a prefix score as a line score. An exhausted budget is a decline, not a best-so-far reported as a best (#1489).

- **`grep`'s `… (+N chars)` named no way to recover the line it cut.** That is what a caller wants precisely when the cut line is the `edit` anchor they came for, and the run that filed #1489 dropped to `python3` to read the raw bytes. A `note:` above the results now gives the call — `read:PATH:LINE-LINE` returns the whole line byte-exactly — with the first cut line's own coordinates as the example. The read render was suspected of being lossy and is not: measured byte-identical to raw on files from 500 to 20000 chars with tabs, trailing spaces and unicode, so the remedy existed and was simply not written where the truncation happens (#1489).

- **`read:PATH:OFFSET:LIMIT` disclosed the OFFSET:LIMIT grammar twice, the second time under the output it would have saved.** [#1432](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1432) moved this disclosure above the body; a trailing `note:` from #382 stayed behind and repeated it, after the reader had already paid for the 60-line window it was warning them about. #382's gate is unchanged and still decides which of the two guesses is offered — it now writes its clause into the `window:` line instead of a second paragraph of its own (#1489).

- **`restartMcp` server names reached a supertool-authored status line unflattened.** A name is an arbitrary JSON string and `', '.join` would put a line of its choosing at column 0 — a second `mcp: restarted 9 daemon(s)` the tool never said. Lower severity than #1583's five payload-key sites, since these come from `.supertool.json`, which #146's trust note places at the same level as the validators and custom ops declared beside them; routed through the same `_flat_keys` anyway, because the alternative is one call cheaper and leaves every future reader to re-derive why (#1489).
