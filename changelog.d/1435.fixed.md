- **A quoted pattern searched the quote characters, and the zero said `scanned 1 files`** ([#1435](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1435)). `grep:'beta':q.txt` returned `(0 results in 0 files, scanned 1 files)` while `grep:beta:q.txt` returned the line — the `'` were matched as literal text. Every clause was true, and `scanned N files` is precisely the disclosure that tells a reader the op *looked*, so the strongest available statement of an absence was made about the pattern and read as one about the file. Quoting a phrase is not a mistake anyone gets warned out of; it is what a shell user does with a pattern containing a space.

  **Nothing is stripped and nothing is refused.** A caller hunting a genuinely quoted phrase — quoted JSON, a quoted word in prose — typed exactly what they meant, and their result is unchanged: no note is printed when the search matched. What changes is only the silence after a zero, and it changes in three states rather than two. The unwrapped spelling is probed over the same population the zero was about, so the render says either `Without them, 'beta' DOES match here — this zero is about the quotes` or `Without them, 'nowhere' matches nothing here either — the quotes are not why this is zero`. The second state is what stops the note becoming noise: a deliberate quoted search looking at a real absence is told so, instead of being sent to re-run a query that comes back just as empty.

  **Only a matched pair counts.** `'zzz'|'yyy'` begins and ends with `'` and is not one — unwrapping it yields a pattern nobody typed — so a further occurrence of the quote character disqualifies it, as does an empty body.

  Swept across the surfaces that share the concept rather than fixed at the entry point that was filed: `grep`, `grep` with context (`grep_around`), `grep`'s count-only mode, `around`, and `read:PATH:::grep=`. Two known divergences are left standing and filed rather than folded in: `between:re:START:END:PATH` reaches none of the shared pattern machinery at all — not this note, not the BRE rewrite, not the saturation refusal — and `gh-job:N:grep:PATTERN` runs in a preset process with its own reader.
