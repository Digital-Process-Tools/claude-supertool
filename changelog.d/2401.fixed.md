- **`serialize_once`'s staleness-reclamation branch no longer treats a still-running holder near its own timeout budget as abandoned** ([#2401](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/2401)). The check compared a lock file's mtime age directly against `timeout_s`, the same number `tests/test_validators_go_vet_669.py` and `tests/test_go_vet_stall_1461.py` both pass as the `go vet` subprocess's own spawn timeout (`GO_WARMUP_S`) -- so a holder legitimately still working, near that shared budget, aged past `timeout_s` at essentially the same real moment its own subprocess would time out, looking identical to a crashed holder from mtime age alone. A waiter could then unlink the still-live lock and take over as the new holder while the original was genuinely still inside `fn`, corrupting more than just running `fn` twice: the original holder's own eventual `finally`-block unlink could go on to remove a completely different, unrelated caller's fresh lock file. `serialize_once` now takes a separate `stale_after_s` budget (new optional parameter, defaulting to `timeout_s * STALE_MARGIN` with `STALE_MARGIN = 3.0`) decoupled from the per-call wait timeout, so a holder within its own declared budget is never mistaken for one that crashed. Reproduced directly with a real two-thread race before the fix (a lock-guarded flag caught the waiter unlinking the holder's still-live lock while the holder was genuinely active) and confirmed fixed after; a genuinely abandoned lock past the new, larger margin is still reclaimed immediately, not waited out. Surfaced by a self-review (Explore) pass on #2398's PR, as explicitly out of scope for that fix.
