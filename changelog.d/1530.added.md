- **`git-push:budget=SECONDS`** ([#1530](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1530)). The push had a fixed 300s budget and no way to ask for more, which on any repository whose pre-push hook runs a test suite makes a push to the default branch impossible rather than slow. Measured live: `FAIL (302.86s)` on a one-line docs commit, against a full suite that took 530.71s and 288.17s in two worktrees the same night. The three routes that existed were each wrong differently — `:no-verify` skips the gate the hook exists to be and is the flag a caller reaches for because it is the only one that helps; raw `git push` is refused by the shipped guard, which names this op as the remedy; a retry costs another 300s and fails again. It bites hardest during a release, where the version-bump commit goes to `master` by definition.

    **Refused, never clamped.** An unreadable value, a non-positive one, two `budget=` tokens that disagree, or anything above the 1800s ceiling stops the op before it pushes, naming the token and why — the same contract an unknown flag has had since [#647](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/647). A budget quietly rounded down to 300 is that defect wearing this one's clothes: the caller believes they asked for twenty minutes and the clock cuts in five. The ceiling exists because the budget has to stay strictly under `ops.git-push.timeout` (raised to 1920s to hold it), or a push killed by supertool's outer cap cannot ask the remote what landed ([#399](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/399)).

    **Not self-sizing, deliberately.** The op can see that a hook would run and knows the destination ref, so it could size itself from "protected branch plus a hook exists" — but it cannot see what any hook *does*, and "master means the full suite" is this repository's convention, not a property of git. A guess that is low is the original defect with extra machinery; one that is high makes a genuinely hung push wait out somebody else's suite length. What decides the number is the load on the caller's machine at the moment they push.

    The timeout receipt is unchanged in substance — it still reads the remote back, refuses to infer success from a clock, names the pre-push hook as the likely consumer of the time, and forbids a force-push on a timeout alone. It gains the remedy it lacked and now reports the budget that actually cut rather than interpolating the default.
