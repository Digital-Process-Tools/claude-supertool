- **The core said `presets/_digits.py` holds the identical digit predicate and nothing compared them** ([#1765](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1765)). `_supertool.py`'s comment states that the preset module "holds the identical pair" and is deliberately not imported — presets run as standalone subprocesses with only `presets/` on their own path, so sharing one module would mean shipping the core into every preset. The duplication is right and stays; the claim was simply unpinned. Both are `^[0-9]+\Z` today, so this is drift exposure rather than a live disagreement: if they part, the core and a preset disagree about what a number is at two layers of one call and no leg goes red. The pin mirrors `tests/test_mcp_autospawn_honoured_1743.py`, which pins the one other core/preset pair claimed by a comment in the same delta — and the asymmetry was the reason this was worth an issue rather than a drive-by, because a reader had no way to tell the pinned pair from the merely-claimed one. `pattern` and `flags` are compared as live objects rather than against a literal written twice, since a third copy would be one more thing to keep in step. Two identically-wrong regexes would satisfy an equality assertion on its own, so the test also pins both sides to the ASCII behaviour: `2` and `1764` match, and `٢`, `²`, `2 `, a trailing newline, the empty string and `1.0` do not. Proved by mutating one side to `^[0-9]+$` — the exact [#1188](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/1188) regression, where Python's `$` also matches before a final newline — and watching the comparison fail.
