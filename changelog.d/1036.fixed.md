- An adapter could switch off `rollback_on_fail` by printing `"timeout": true`
  in its own result. The core reads that flag to mean "the checker never
  answered", which since #969 correctly suppresses rollback — but the flag is
  the core's own, stamped by the arm that watched a subprocess exceed its
  budget, and nothing stopped an adapter from setting it. A real finding wearing
  it rendered as `NOT CHECKED` and the bad write stood. Every key the core owns
  (`timeout`, `no_verdict`, `elapsed_s`, `resolved_to`) is now dropped from an
  adapter's payload at the one point it enters the core, and the adapter's own
  `ok` / `count` / `errors` are left untouched. Its message also no longer
  prints under the `orchestrator` provenance column, which exists to mark text
  the core wrote.
