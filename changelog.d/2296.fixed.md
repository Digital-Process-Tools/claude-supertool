- **A GitLab runner that fails every job it accepts — disk full, `prepare_executor` dying in 4-10s — was invisible to the `gl-runners` watcher** ([#2296](https://github.com/Digital-Process-Tools/claude-supertool/issues/2296)). Its nine events were all about fleet membership and the pending queue; none read how a job *ended*, so a runner that stays `online`, never pauses, never leaves the fleet and drains its queue fast enough to never starve it raised nothing. Two new events close the gap: `runner_failing_systemically` when a runner crosses `_SYSTEMIC_FAILURE_THRESHOLD` (3) jobs ending in GitLab's own `failure_reason: "runner_system_failure"` inside the 30-minute window `annotate_recent_work` already fetches for its throughput count — no second API call, no new state — and `runner_recovered_systemically` once the count actually reaches zero and the runner completes at least one job since; a window merely aging out with no new activity is silence, not recovery, and is not reported as one (self-review caught and fixed a first cut that gated on the threshold rather than on zero, which could fire "recovered" on a runner still failing every job it completed).
